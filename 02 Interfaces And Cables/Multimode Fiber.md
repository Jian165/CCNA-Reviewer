# 🌈 Multimode Fiber

> [!abstract] In one line
> **Multimode fiber (MMF)** has a **wider core** that lets **multiple angles (modes) of light** travel through it at once — longer reach than UTP, but shorter than [[Single-Mode Fiber|single-mode]].

## 🔦 Multiple Modes of Light

In multimode fiber, the **core** — the actual glass fiber — is **wider** than in single-mode fiber. This wider core allows **multiple angles**, known as **modes**, of light waves to enter the fiberglass core.

- The red and black lines in the diagrams represent **light waves** travelling down the fiberglass core.
- They reflect off the **cladding** at **different angles**.

> [!example] Think of it as...
> A wide hallway: several people can travel down it at different angles at the same time.

## 📏 Distance and Cost

| Cable | Distance | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **UTP** | Up to **100 m** | Cheapest |
| **Multimode fiber** | Longer than UTP, shorter than single-mode (**400–550 m** in the standards shown) | Cheaper than single-mode |
| **Single-mode fiber** | Longest (**5–30 km**) | Most expensive |

Multimode fiber cables are cheaper than single-mode cables because they use **cheaper transmitters**, which are often **LED-based**.

> [!success] Key takeaway
> **Wider core → multiple light modes → LED-based transmitters**: multimode reaches farther than UTP but not as far as single-mode, and it costs less than single-mode.
