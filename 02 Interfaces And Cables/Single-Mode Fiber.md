# 🎯 Single-Mode Fiber

> [!abstract] In one line
> **Single-mode fiber (SMF)** has a **narrow core** that carries **one angle (mode) of light** from a **laser-based transmitter**, supporting the **longest cable distances**.

## 🔬 Narrow Core

The **core diameter** of a single-mode fiber cable is **narrower** than multimode fiber — the glass fiber is thinner. The center represents the **fiberglass core**, and the blue represents the **reflective cladding**.

You can notice the difference compared with the [[Multimode Fiber|multimode]] diagrams.

## 🔦 One Mode of Light

- Light enters at a **single angle** — one **mode** — from a **laser-based transmitter**.
- The light wave travels **straight down the core** of the cable instead of reflecting at many angles.

> [!example] Think of it as...
> A narrow tunnel: only one straight path is possible.

## 📏 Distance and Cost

| Feature | Single-Mode Fiber | Multimode Fiber |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Core** | Narrow | Wider |
| **Modes of light** | One | Many |
| **Transmitter** | Laser-based (more expensive) | LED-based (cheaper) |
| **Distance** | Longest — **5–30 km** in the standards shown | Shorter — **400–550 m** |
| **Cost** | More expensive | Cheaper |

> [!success] Key takeaway
> **Narrow core → one light mode → laser-based transmitter**: single-mode fiber supports the longest distances but costs more than multimode.
