# 🧵 UTP Cable

> [!abstract] In one line
> The copper cable used by Ethernet standards is **UTP — Unshielded Twisted Pair** — four twisted pairs of wire (**8 wires**) ending in an **RJ-45** connector.

## 📖 What UTP Means

| Part | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Unshielded** | The wires have **no metallic shield**, making them vulnerable to electrical interference |
| **Twisted Pair** | Pairs of wires are **twisted together** to help protect against interference |

![[Pasted image 20261114143733.png]]

## 🌀 Why the Twist Matters

The **twist** helps protect against **electromagnetic interference (EMI)**. A UTP cable contains **four pairs** of wire twisted together, which makes **eight wires** in total.

![[Pasted image 20261114143942.png]]

## 🔌 RJ-45 Connector

The **RJ-45** connector has **8 pins** — one for each of the **8 wires** in a UTP cable.

![[Pasted image 20261112141834.png]]

## 🧮 Pairs Used by Standard

| Standards | Pairs Used | Wires Used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **10BASE-T** and **100BASE-T** | 2 pairs | 4 wires |
| **1000BASE-T** and **10GBASE-T** | 4 pairs | 8 wires |

> [!warning] Important
> 10BASE-T and 100BASE-T are also known as **Ethernet** and **Fast Ethernet** cables and use only **2 pairs (4 wires)**. Higher-speed standards use **all 4 pairs (8 wires)**.

> [!success] Key takeaway
> UTP = **Unshielded Twisted Pair**: **4 pairs / 8 wires**, twisted to fight **EMI**, terminated in an **8-pin RJ-45**.
