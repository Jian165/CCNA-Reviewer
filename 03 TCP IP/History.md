# 📜 History of TCP/IP

> [!abstract] In one line
> TCP/IP grew from **ARPANET** (online in **1969**, originally using **NCP**). **Vint Cerf** and **Bob Kahn** began developing **TCP in 1974**; it was later split into **TCP + IP**, and ARPANET fully adopted it on **1 January 1983**.

## 🌱 Origins: ARPANET

> [!info] Where it started
> Early work on the computer networks that would evolve into today's Internet began in the **1960s**, mainly in the **US**.

- The US Department of Defense's **ARPA** — **Advanced Research Projects Agency** — funded **ARPANET**.
- ARPANET came online in **1969**, connecting **mainframes** at universities and labs around the country.
- ARPANET originally used a protocol called **NCP** — **Network Control Program**.
- There was **no TCP/IP** in the early days.

## 🧬 From NCP to TCP/IP

- In **1974**, **Vint Cerf** and **Bob Kahn** — two of the fathers of the modern Internet — began developing **TCP**, originally the **Transmission Control Program**, as an **internetworking protocol**.
- TCP went through several revisions and was later **divided into two protocols** that are still used today:
  - **TCP** — Transmission Control **Protocol** (note the name change from "Program" to "Protocol")
  - **IP** — **Internet Protocol**
- Together these two protocols form the foundation of the protocol suite widely known as **TCP/IP**.

## 📅 Timeline

| Year | Event |
| --- | --- |
| **1960s** | Early network research begins in the US |
| **1969** | **ARPANET** comes online, funded by ARPA (US DoD) |
| Early days | ARPANET uses **NCP** — no TCP/IP yet |
| **1974** | **Cerf and Kahn** begin developing **TCP** (Transmission Control Program) |
| After revisions | TCP splits into **TCP** and **IP** |
| **1 January 1983** | ARPANET fully switches to **TCP/IP** |
| Today | TCP/IP is still the foundation of the Internet |

## 🏆 Why TCP/IP Won

> [!success] Open standards beat proprietary solutions
> Over time TCP/IP became dominant over other vendor-proprietary solutions of the day because:
> - It was published as a set of **open standards** that **any vendor could implement**.
> - It could run over **many different types of networks**.
>
> Several decades later, here we are still learning about TCP/IP.

> [!tip] Memory hook
> **ARPA** funded **ARPANET** → **NCP** came first → **Cerf + Kahn** created **TCP** → split into **TCP/IP** → **1983** cutover → **open standards** won.
